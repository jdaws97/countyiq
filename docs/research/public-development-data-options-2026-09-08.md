## Priority acquisition recommendation

1. **Implement a low-volume, evidence-first public-document watch.** Monitor County bid postings and Commissioners agendas/minutes, York City council/HARB/planning pages, and Springettsbury's posted permit report. Save the official document URL, meeting date, project address/parcel, action verb, counterparty, amount, and source snapshot. This yields early project and award signals with no subscription or RTK request.
2. **Use parcel-first recorded-document research only after a project is identified.** Query the free assessment/property viewer, then the official Recorder-directed index/Webstore pathway. Purchase/download only the small set of deeds, easements, plans, and agreement records needed to support a relationship. The County supports free in-office searching, but its no-camera policy means an operational workflow should budget lawful printed copies or official online image charges rather than screen capture.
3. **Add a confidence model for utilities.** “Served by/territory” is weak; “application filed” is preliminary; an executed agreement, approved planning module/resolution, recorded easement, or PUC order is stronger; completed service remains a separate state. Store capacity as unknown unless an authoritative record states quantity, system, and condition.
4. **Do not build a countywide permit-history ingestion on the evidence presently available.** DCED establishes reporting of aggregate counts but not a public historic results feed; York City establishes online submission, not public historic search. Start with City meeting artifacts and targeted municipalities where official searchable histories are later verified. Otherwise record the gap rather than filling it with paid aggregators or assumptions.

York County has strong public building blocks for parcel anchoring, recorded transactions, and public-government procurement. The most realistic inexpensive pipeline is project discovery from official meeting/procurement documents, followed by targeted Recorder research and careful utility-status evidence. It will not create complete countywide permit starts or utility-capacity coverage: those public, machine-usable sources were not verified. Keep the product explicit about those missing records and provenance rather than treating a portal's ability to accept an application—or a utility's general service area—as proof of approval, capacity, construction, or beneficial ownership.
