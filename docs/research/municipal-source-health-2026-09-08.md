## Collector implications

The current scraper deliberately stops after ordinary HTTP errors; robots HTTP
403 does not enter its browser-robots path. Manheim, North York, and
Springettsbury show why a transport error is different from a verified robots
disallow. A reviewed ordinary-browser path should establish the actual robots
response and preserve genuine restrictions, rather than treating unavailable
robots information as permission.

The extractor handles PDF/DOC/DOCX links and selected download routes. It does
not traverse arbitrary meeting-detail pages or retain HTML minutes. Its Drive
folder expression omits `embeddedfolderview?id=`. The shared-listing body router
accepts all documents when only one body is configured for that URL; Peach
Bottom shows why visible section/body information matters too.

Five exact configured pages still carry their historical broken links: Dover,
Lower Windsor, Manchester, North Codorus, and Spring Grove. These are partial
coverage defects, even while useful collection succeeds. A blanket green status
would conceal them, but a single red source-family gauge overstates their
equivalence to a total collection outage.

No claim is made that every meeting's minutes are posted online. The four
unconfirmed body-specific sources need archive confirmation or direct record
acquisition. These findings support substantial recovery without assuming that
a failed source contains no public information.
