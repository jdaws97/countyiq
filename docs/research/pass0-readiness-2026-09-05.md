## What the failures taught us

The first stress run retained 19 of 26 useful matters (73.1%) and had 17 of 21 supported discovery entries (81.0%). The revised prompt recovered omitted infrastructure and policy topics, but model relevance labels remained too permissive. Unspecified grants, builder names, maintenance issues, and contract payments could become opportunities despite accurate narrative text.

The consequential change is a source-based discovery qualification step. Model category labels alone no longer establish physical development scope or a substantive land-use subject. Ambiguous records remain context where valid; they are not promoted into opportunities. The checks are conservative and may withhold unfamiliar legitimate scope for review.

Other changes:

- Clarified complete-document coverage, short late infrastructure entries, preliminary rezoning, exact identity text and passage IDs, district labels, and concise narratives.
- Distinguished financial/administrative project history from construction contracts and identified capital grants using validated action wording.
- Preserved failed and tied votes, correct deciding bodies, conditional approvals, historical dates, and pending future consideration.
- Normalized a recommendation carrying a source-validated completed advisory referral vote to a procedural decision. Failed votes, invented wording, scheduled items, and substantive ordinance approvals cannot use that normalization.
- Preserved short policy headings and their explicitly subordinate entries, including an index split between a numbered heading and its lettered child. Added source passage IDs and exact evidence remain auditable; neighboring main entries cannot lend scope.
- Retained exact-quote and critical-identity rejection. Optional unsupported organization names can be withheld separately with original claims and rejection reasons preserved.
