# York County, PA public-data discovery: incremental sources

Research date: September 8, 2026. This pass supplements, rather than repeats, the existing CountyIQ York dataset register and public-data landscape. It prioritizes records that were actually reachable, downloadable, or queryable in this review. “Free” means no payment or account was encountered for viewing; automated reuse/bulk terms are unknown unless stated otherwise.

## Sources checked but not promoted as a record feed

No project-address permit/status-search example was verified in this pass for York Township, Conewago Township, or Pennsylvania Manchester Township. Hanover Borough’s official 2020 permit summary is aggregate monthly activity/value, not a current address-level history. A York-specific construction-NPDES result was not verified through the eFACTS map endpoint; the separate PA Bulletin final-action table does supply York examples below. A complete countywide construction-permit history remains a gap.

The County’s configured OPEN_DATA parcel, zoning, footprint, address, subdivision, growth, future-use, easement, soil and park layers; PennDOT baseline layers; and existing tax/sheriff workflows are deliberately not re-catalogued. The County Property Tax Viewer is a restricted surface and should not be scraped.

## Evidence examples

The [PA Bulletin’s published final-action table](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pabulletin/data/vol55/55-40/1356b.html) records a Chapter 102 Individual NPDES permit **issued** to Core5 Ind Partners LLC in Manchester Borough, York County (application PAD670033). This is a free official, dated permit-action lead; it does not name a street address on that notice and issuance is not proof that site work began or remains active.

The [Northeastern York County Sewer Authority’s April 27, 2026 minutes](https://nesewer.org/document/meeting-minutes-april-27th-2026/) record unanimous approval of contractor change orders for the Musser Run Pump Station Improvements. Its [May 18, 2026 minutes](https://nesewer.org/document/meeting-minutes-may-18th-2026/) report that a restriction of three new Saginaw connections annually was expected to continue while the Authority prioritized a Sherman Street pump-station upgrade. These are project-specific public actions and a localized connection constraint, not a commitment to a particular private development, completed construction, or a systemwide capacity determination.

## Recommended use

Start with Springettsbury and York City Planning Commission because they provide the clearest dated project evidence. Add Northeastern York County Sewer Authority only for its identified participating area and use its minutes to confirm—not infer—utility commitments. Join a permit/meeting signal to the existing parcel identity, then seek a recorded easement/deed, authority action, or public procurement record before presenting a relationship as fact. Use DEP and PennDOT records as bounded corroboration and public-infrastructure context. Maintain a source URL, retrieval date, document date, issuer, exact quoted field/value, and a confidence state for every finding; do not convert a permit, application, grant, or bid into an actual start, completed project, capacity finding, or ownership conclusion.

## Existing data versus acquisition work

A read-only check of the isolated local source copy found 26,906 current planning-subdivision rows, including 417 with received dates since 2025, 2,615 with an engineer value and 238 with a proposed-new-area value. The latest received date was August 24, 2026. The current intelligence packet already includes nearby planning records with engineer and proposed-development fields. Use and improve those joins before building a duplicate county planning collector; neither a nearby plan nor a repeated engineer establishes common ownership or shared project scope.

That copy also contains 78 current PennDOT project rows, 1,314 DEP cleanup rows and 384 DEP tank rows, but no `pa_open_data` records. Configuration is not evidence of populated coverage. Cleanup/tank records are not construction-permit actions; the new PA Bulletin source would add a distinct event family. These counts describe the inspected local copy, not independently checked production coverage.

Recommended acquisition order:

1. Springettsbury permit reports and PA Bulletin York permit actions: independent address or applicant-level progress evidence.
2. Sewer/water authority documents: commitments, limits, engineering scope, contractor selection and changes. Start with the verified Northeastern authority and expand by documented service area.
3. County, City, authority and PennDOT procurement: actual solicitation/award dates and responsible organizations; retain closed records.
4. RACP and other project grants, WARN notices, redevelopment-authority dispositions: funding and contraction signals that can support or weaken a development case.
5. PJM, PUC and issuer disclosures: targeted corroboration for already identified infrastructure or company hypotheses, with current-status checks.

For each candidate collector, first retain a small dated sample, measure useful York project matches against existing records, review access terms, and demonstrate a new supported fact or a useful abstention. Then implement the recurring collection. None of the research above has been silently added to the customer evidence pool.
