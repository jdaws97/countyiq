# Permit data and intelligence competitors — official-page check

Checked September 8, 2026. This is a product-claim comparison, not an independent coverage or accuracy test. “Nationwide,” project counts, update frequency, and prediction claims below are each vendor-reported; none establishes coverage of every municipality or a current record for a particular York County project. No conclusion about CountyIQ’s relative quality, price, or ability to win a market follows from these pages.

## Construction Monitor

Official pages: [single-family permit data](https://shop.constructionmonitor.com/single-family-permit-data/) and [platform overview](https://www.constructionmonitor.com/main/).

It explicitly sells permit data as leads. It claims 50-state/nationwide coverage, 3,000+ jurisdictions, daily updates, 30+ years of history, and 31M+ total permits. These are self-reported aggregate claims, not a municipal coverage list.

This goes beyond showing a permit: the page describes address/geocode, owner and contractor details, valuation, status, filters, permit mapping, real-time leads, top-company reports, statistics, API/FTP, and CRM use. It is primarily a prospecting and sales-workflow product; the stated buyers are suppliers, trade contractors, GCs, and homebuilders. The reviewed public pages do not state a price. No official-page evidence of predictive scoring or generative AI was found.

## BuildZoom

Official page: [National Building Permit Database](https://www.buildzoom.com/blog/guides/national-building-permit-database).

BuildZoom says its database spans 3.7M contractor license records and hundreds of millions of permits from thousands of U.S. jurisdictions. That is self-reported; the page does not promise all jurisdictions or publish a current coverage roster.

It describes permit scope, valuation, contractor, and location data; a Property Map for permit histories; research requests using project/contractor/business keywords and value filters; and automated permit alerts. It also says those records help it recommend contractors. Thus it has enrichment, filtering, and alerting, although the reviewed page does not claim construction-stage prediction, owner-entity resolution, or AI. Its visible scope includes its consumer contractor marketplace and reporter/research access; enterprise pricing is not disclosed on the page.

## Dodge Construction Network

Official pages: [Dodge One / project discovery](https://www.construction.com/find-projects-first/), [Dodge Report](https://www.construction.com/what-is-the-dodge-report/), and [general-contractor solution](https://www.construction.com/general-contractors/).

Dodge says its U.S. commercial project data follows pre-planning/design through permits and construction start, combining automated municipal/proprietary-source monitoring with 400+ field specialists. It claims 7,000+ daily published updates and 10,000 project additions/updates a day. Those are vendor claims, and they do not establish universal municipal permit coverage.

This is strongly intelligence-oriented: field-verified project profiles with location, stage, estimated value, contacts and specifications; early-stage alerts; personalized recommendations; forecasts; relationship/firm information; analytics; CRM synchronization; API/automation and opportunity scoring. It lists GCs, subcontractors, architects/engineers, distributors, developers/owners, and building-product manufacturers as users. Public pricing is not stated; the pages request a demo. The solutions page lists “AI Search Assist,” but the reviewed pages do not explain its model, inputs, or reliability.

## LightBox

Official pages: [product catalog](https://www.lightboxre.com/products/) and [company/product overview](https://www.lightboxre.com/).

LightBox advertises nationwide property/location data: parcels, structures, addresses, zoning, transaction history, building footprints, True Owner and Corporate Owner, delivered through mapping, APIs, bulk delivery, feature services and Snowflake. It describes analytics, location intelligence, document intelligence, and connected CRE workflows for CRE, lending/debt/equity, infrastructure, government, and development customers.

That is meaningful property-record enrichment and identity/context infrastructure. On the current official pages reviewed, LightBox does **not** explicitly claim a building-permit feed, permit-stage monitoring, construction-lead alerts, or a construction-start prediction product. Pricing is not disclosed. Reonomy is now an Altus Group product, so its present public site is evaluated separately rather than assumed to be a LightBox capability.

## Reonomy (property/investor-intelligence alternative)

Official page: [Reonomy](https://www.reonomy.com/).

Reonomy says it combines title/assessor, geospatial, demographic, debt/lien, corporate-ownership, and transaction records; it reports 53M+ commercial properties across all U.S. states. It explicitly uses machine learning to associate records with properties and resolve a “true owner,” and offers a likelihood-to-sell score based on tenure, debt, transaction history and market signals. These are vendor claims, not independently tested results.

Its intelligence is entity resolution, portfolio/contact enrichment, off-market targeting, transaction/debt history, filtering, APIs, and predictive sell likelihood—not a stated permit product. Buyers are CRE professionals and commercial service providers. Reonomy publicly says plans start at **$400/month on an annual subscription**; bulk/API pricing is not disclosed.

## What this evidence can and cannot answer

The official pages show that permit-derived products can be turned into enriched, filtered, alerted, and sometimes forecast/scored workflows; merely publishing permit records is not the only market model. Dodge is the clearest direct commercial-construction intelligence comparator. Construction Monitor and BuildZoom demonstrate permit-led prospecting and alerting. LightBox and Reonomy demonstrate adjacent property/owner identity and investment intelligence, while the reviewed pages do not establish that either supplies permit feeds.

Whether CountyIQ can compete remains unproven by marketing pages. A defensible answer needs a comparable, local evaluation of source coverage/freshness, identity-resolution accuracy, stage/contract evidence, alert usefulness, buyer workflow, and price—not competitors’ advertised aggregate scale.
